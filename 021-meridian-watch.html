The calibre, described without adjectives

It is a twin-barrel movement, which means two mainsprings sit side by side rather than one behind the other. The result is a longer, flatter power curve: the watch holds its rate for five minutes when fully wound and still holds it on the last turn of the crown.

There is no rotor. A watch that has not been worn for two days will not have kept the time while it sat in a drawer, and a hand wound watch never pretends otherwise.

The bridge is finished by hand with a single tool called a black-polish steel: a flat tin charged with emery, worked in a figure-eight until the surface has no measurable reflection at all. It takes four hours and it cannot be undone.

Each movement is timed in six positions and on a rocking arm. The result is written on a card and signed, and the card is folded into the case with the watch.
