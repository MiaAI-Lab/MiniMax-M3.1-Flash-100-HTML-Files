Tide
01 / 04 — Surface
Chapter one

The sea is not quiet. It is speaking at a frequency we mistake for silence.

Stand at the edge for ten minutes and the sound resolves: a low swell with a period of eight seconds, a hiss of breaking over the sand bar, and beneath both a slow undertow that never stops moving.

Chapter two

Every wave is a record of the wind that made it.

Fetch a beach on a stormy afternoon and you can read the weather that arrived hours earlier — fetch length becomes wave height, wave period becomes swell length. The beach is a logbook.

Chapter three

Beneath the break, the water falls for kilometres.

The continental shelf ends in a slope. Water arriving over it converts its horizontal push into a downward slide — up to four kilometres of vertical drop in a few kilometres of travel, and the only place it surfaces is in the roar of surf.

Chapter four

Then silence, and the slowest clock in the world.

Below two thousand metres there is no light, no wave and no year. A signal sent into that column takes three hours to return. The abyss keeps time in a way no other place on Earth does.
