DESCRIPTION:
A sundial that actually works, computed from spherical astronomy rather than drawn. The declination comes from the Sun's ecliptic longitude, the shadow is laid along the anti-solar azimuth with its length set by the tangent of the altitude, and the hour lines are each solved individually — which is why they bunch towards noon and spread towards the horizon instead of sitting in tidy thirty-degree sectors. The analemma is traced from the same equations at a fixed nine o'clock.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Solar declination from ecliptic longitude and the obliquity of the ecliptic
- Spherical-triangle altitude and azimuth with the hour angle
- Shadow direction and length from the tangent of the altitude
- Analemma traced across 365 days at a fixed hour
- Equation of time from the standard four-term series
- Sunrise and sunset from the hour-angle cosine
