DESCRIPTION:
Iron filings, computed rather than photographed. Every filing on the page is a short line drawn in the local direction of the magnetic field, with its opacity set by the strength of the field — which is exactly what a real filing is, since each one is a tiny compass needle that swivels to align. The field itself is the sum of inverse-square contributions from point poles at the ends of each bar magnet, so the shape of the pattern is the shape of the real field, and the bare wedge between two like poles is genuine.

MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Two-point-pole representation of a bar magnet with inverse-square field
- Filings drawn as a grid of short segments aligned with the local field
- Opacity mapped to field strength, mimicking a real scatter of filings
- Streamline integration for the field-line overlay
- Null-point detection by sampling the field on a grid
- Gradient-shaded magnet bodies with coloured poles

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Arrange the magnets. Drag them, double-click to reverse a polarity, add more, turn on the field lines, and read the field at the centre of the page.
