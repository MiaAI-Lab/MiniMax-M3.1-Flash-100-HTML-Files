The method

Make a world out of flat things

Cut paper dioramas have been made for four hundred years. The technique is unchanged: draw the nearest shape, cut it, mount it a few millimetres in front of the next, and let the gap do the work of depth.

01
Draw the nearest edge

Only the silhouette of the closest layer is visible, so only the silhouette needs to be beautiful. Everything else can be implied.

02
Tear, do not cut

A torn edge keeps the fibre of the sheet and catches light unevenly. It reads as landscape; a cut edge reads as paper.

03
Mount with a gap

Four millimetres between layers is enough. The eye supplies the rest, and the world becomes as deep as you are patient enough to build.

04
Light from one side

A single warm source at a low angle turns seven flat shapes into a valley, a forest and a sky.
