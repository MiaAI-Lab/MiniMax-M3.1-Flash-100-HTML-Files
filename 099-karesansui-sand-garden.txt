DESCRIPTION:
A dry garden you can actually rake. The sand is a height field with an angle of repose, so a steep cut avalanches and fills itself in while a shallow one keeps its edge — and dragging the rake lowers a line and pushes the displaced sand out to either side, which is what the teeth of a real rake do. Sweep the garden and the pattern comes out around every stone.
MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Height field of 220 by 112 cells for the sand
- Rake displacement that lowers a line and pushes sand out to the sides
- Angle-of-repose relaxation between neighbouring cells
- Raking light from the upper left giving every groove a highlight and a shadow
- Stones drawn as shaded ellipsoids that the sand cannot cross
- Concentric rake sweeps generated around every stone

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Rake it. Drag across the sand to carve, sweep the whole garden, wet it to stop the sand moving, and reset it when the pattern has gone.
