Caldera
Volcán Tecalate · Guatemala

It has not erupted
since 1974,
and it is thinking about it.

A volcano is a conversation between pressure and patience. Every day the magma chamber makes a little more gas; every day the rock above it gets a little heavier. One day the conversation ends in an afternoon.

ACT ONE

Dormant

Steam from a fumarole on the north flank. The last eruption put 2.1 km of ash over four villages and nobody was hurt, which surprises people every time.

ACT TWO

Eruption

A column rises eleven kilometres in four hours, and the wind takes it east. The first pyroclastic flow arrives at 03:40 and finishes the job.

ACT THREE

Collapse

The summit falls in on itself. A caldera 1.4 km across forms in ninety seconds, and the mountain is now 400 metres shorter than the map says.
