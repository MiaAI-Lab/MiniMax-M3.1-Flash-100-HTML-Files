DESCRIPTION:
An hourglass, with the heap simulated properly. The sand in the lower bulb is a height field of sixty-four columns that relaxes after every grain according to the angle of repose, so the pile is a cone with a flat top rather than a bump, and it grows by eating its own sides. The flow rate is proportional to the square of the neck width, so a wider neck really does empty faster, and turning the glass over mirrors the heap into the top — which is a lovely little inaccuracy every hourglass has always had.
MAJOR VISUAL TECHNIQUES:
- Height field of sixty-four columns for the heap in the lower bulb
- Angle-of-repose relaxation passing sand downhill between neighbouring columns
- Rake-style displacement that lowers a line and pushes sand out to the sides
- Two glass bulbs built from quadratic curves meeting at the neck
- Stream of falling grains drawn between the bulbs
- Flow rate proportional to the square of the neck width

INTENDED INTERACTION MODEL:
Turn it over. Watch the heap grow, change the neck width and the grain size, slow the pour, and turn the glass over to start the sand again.
